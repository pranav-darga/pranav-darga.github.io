# Product

## Register

brand

## Platform

web

## Users

Primary audience is recruiters and hiring managers evaluating Pranav Darga for full-time software engineering roles. They arrive with limited attention, skimming many candidates' portfolios, and are judging both stated credentials and demonstrated skill in the same visit.

## Product Purpose

A personal portfolio for Pranav Darga, a software engineer. Its purpose isn't just to list credentials — the site itself is meant to function as a work sample. Success means a recruiter leaves believing this person can genuinely build things, backed by real, checkable project work, and reaches out.

## Positioning

The site itself is the proof. Its own polish, interaction quality, and code craft demonstrate frontend skill more persuasively than a written case study could — show, don't tell.

## Conversion & proof

- Primary and secondary CTA: Get in touch (primary), View experience timeline (secondary fallback for visitors not ready to reach out yet).
- The line a visitor remembers after 10 seconds: "This person can really build things."
- Belief ladder: (1) this person has real frontend skill, shown directly by the site's own polish and interaction design; (2) that work is verifiable — live project demo links and GitHub repos back it up, not just claims; (3) they're worth reaching out to. Contact must stay reachable from the header at every point along that journey, not gated behind scrolling to the bottom.
- Proof on hand: Live project demo links and GitHub repository links, to be added by the user in place of the current placeholder project slides. Job history and about-me copy are also still placeholder text pending real content.

## Brand Personality

Confident, precise, modern, minimal, calm, refined. Continues the intuit.com/careers corporate-editorial direction already established in the codebase: generous whitespace, rounded cards, a confident blue accent, restrained motion. Quiet competence over flash.

## Anti-references

Should never read as an unmodified, generic template portfolio — needs a distinct point of view. Avoid gimmicky or overly playful visuals; novelty undercuts credibility with a recruiter audience that's evaluating professional judgment as much as code.

## Design Principles

Show, don't tell — the site's own execution is the strongest evidence of skill on offer, so craft is not optional polish, it's the argument.

Contact stays one click away — the primary CTA (get in touch) must remain reachable from the header at every scroll position, not just at the bottom of the page.

Credibility over cleverness — restraint and consistency read as competence to this audience; any expressive or playful touches earn their place only if they reinforce craft rather than distract from it.

Verifiable over descriptive — link out to live demos and real repositories rather than narrating claims about the work.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

No specific accessibility requirements were named. Apply general good practice (contrast, keyboard navigation, reduced-motion support) as a baseline rather than a formal compliance target.
